# Make the narration and pictures one story

Use for news production and revisions about mechanical narration, excessive cutting or mismatched footage. These checks complement provenance and encoding checks; they are editorial judgments rather than quality scores.

## Write the decision before the script

Choose a viewer and the news point that affects their work: a meaningful release, whether to switch a coding model, which access tier to choose, or what a claimed capability buys. Open with a supported stake and pay it off. A verified release/event/time anchor is welcome when immediately connected to a meaningful change; it does not need to be rewritten as a generic question. A bare announcement or feature list without that change supplies little reason to continue. Avoid invented audience reactions and claims that a video will go viral.

For growth-oriented news, use [attention and payoff](attention-and-payoff.md) to select the angle and pair the opening words with its actual first source frame. Deliver the core news early, then add evidence and useful interpretation; do not save the headline fact for the end. Information progression does not require faster cutting.

Write/revise the full narration with [spoken news writing](spoken-news-writing.md) before splitting scenes. Let the next fact or consequence move the story; do not turn every transition into another question. Keep model versions and the condition needed to understand each comparison; place secondary number lists in visible evidence/source notes. The writing review and the following picture/timing review serve different purposes.

For partial revisions, retain approved passages and inspect the boundary into the revised text. A picture change cannot repair a switch in the video's purpose: a continuous musical performance does not by itself call for a game-engine evaluation or an unperformed navigation test. Related demonstrations and comparisons can enrich the same story when their purpose is clear: show control in use, then distinguish an existing capability from the update. When the user wants more substance, research and connect those additions; simply deleting down to one demonstration and caveats does not satisfy that request.

## Bind each spoken idea to visible material

Before recording/TTS, make a compact map: exact spoken idea → visible source state/result → what the viewer should notice → evidence limit. Select material after deciding the claim. A game clip supports a specific attributed game-development example; it cannot prove general benchmark parity or lower API bills. A price paragraph should show the named models, rate class, currency and unit. Follow the Chinese-audience domestic-price preference; RMB and USD may coexist as clearly marked native prices, but a price paragraph must never silently change currency or retain an invalid cross-currency ratio.

Distinguish factual claims from the author's advice. Advice such as “run the same bug on both models” can accompany the real access/model documentation, but must not look like an already completed comparison. Keep source footage and the spoken claim in the same task; inspect measured speech timing rather than relying on paragraph length. When one source change is essential inside a paragraph, cut after the relevant clause actually ends. Within-scene shot durations are proportional weights in this renderer, not exact spoken-word timestamps; use separate measured speech scenes or verified explicit timing when semantic alignment depends on that boundary.

## Hold a thought; cut when the evidence changes

Use a primary material block for a complete question, comparison or result. For a roughly minute-long explanation, a handful of such blocks is often enough; this is guidance, not a mandatory shot count or universal interval. Preserve reading time for names, numbers and actions. Use a restrained, motivated close view/highlight inside the current source instead of changing pages every few seconds. Keep scroll direction and camera motion purposeful. Do not reset the same source framing at an artificial scene boundary.

Cut when the next statement needs a different source, task or result. Establish where the viewer is, then reveal the relevant evidence. Avoid a release page → game → robot sequence under an undifferentiated “coding capability” sentence. Transitions cannot create a logical connection missing from the script. Do not replace arbitrary fast cuts with arbitrary long holds: if a source has nothing relevant left to show, shorten the narration or select a better source.

## Review the encoded result

Check the opening as a complete segment: is the topic identified and the viewer stake clear? Then inspect a continuous sequence around each cut, with the corresponding narration/subtitle timestamps. Does the previous thought finish, does the incoming picture answer the next thought, and can a phone viewer find the relevant item before it disappears? Review later moments in held shots too; a recording may scroll off the needed row before the voice finishes.

Record concrete problems with timestamps and corrections, including issues the user did not list: unreadable source text, covered numbers, repeated speech, wrong subtitles, incorrect version, unsupported headline, redundant proof, abrupt ending or distracting sound. Use ASR for omissions/numbers/repetition where available; it does not certify vocal naturalness. If audio playback/listening is unavailable, state that limit in the review instead of claiming a listening check. Recheck altered segments in the final encoded file. Report what was fixed and any remaining limitation; material ratios and test counts do not establish engaging content.
