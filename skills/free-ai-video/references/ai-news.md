# AI news: research to a finished short video

Use this for current AI news and trend requests. The goal is an editorial video about developments relevant to viewers. Installed TTS, rendering tools and the production process supply the means; they do not determine the news topic unless the user requests that topic.

## Find and choose the story

1. Collect public nominations with `factory.py collect`, then perform current web searches covering the last 72 hours and expand to seven days if needed. Include Chinese-language coverage, official company/lab announcements and developer/community discussion. RSS or GitHub scores are a partial source pool, not evidence that something is popular. Retain failed sources and use web search to fill the gaps.
2. When the user asks for discovery, compare a small shortlist on freshness, observable discussion/coverage, relevance to viewers, a concrete hook and useful available visuals. Record the actual signal/URL; don't invent views, rankings or viral probabilities. A definite user-supplied topic can go directly to research; examples they offer are leads to investigate.
3. Read the selected story's original announcement/model card/docs. Separate announcement time, service rollout and promised future weights. Attribute vendor benchmarks and demo outcomes to the vendor. Treat forum opinions as discussion signals, not verified capability. Preserve conflicting dates/time zones in research; use the original local publication date in the video when available.

The target audience here is Chinese customers: prioritize original Chinese/domestic pages for Chinese providers and domestic RMB access/pricing. Foreign providers retain official native prices. Apply [audience region and native prices](model-launch.md#audience-region-and-native-prices); keep language, billing region and currency as separate facts. News openings may lead with a confirmed release date or relevant event, followed by the change that gives viewers a reason to continue.

## Research record

Set `content_type: ai_news` and `research_file: research.json` in project.json. The JSON contains `recorded_at`, `searches` (query plus actual `result_urls`), `candidates` (id, title, date, primary_sources and selection observations), `selected_id`, `selection_reason`, `claim_checks` (claim plus source_url, consistent with project sources) and `media_plan`. Record the searches that were actually run; a template filled with hypothetical URLs is not research. The factory checks this record before audio/video creation and copies it to the finished folder. Syntax checks do not replace the agent reading and verifying sources.

Also deliver a short Chinese selection note linking the shortlist and selected angle, so the user can see the research and editorial choice. Research what happened, its relevance, the change, evidence and current availability; do not read those research questions as spoken section headings. Write and revise using [spoken news writing](spoken-news-writing.md), keeping `script.md` and a short `script-review.md` in the job. Those are host-authored editing records, not CLI-generated proof of writing quality. Put a concrete point in the opening, then use a few useful details rather than a catalog of specifications.

For model releases, apply [model-launch.md](model-launch.md) before writing. Include `editorial_brief` in research.json, with the audience and question, chosen hook and supporting claim IDs, capability and pricing checks, competitor selection, a meaningful limitation and the practical takeaway. Include both a relevant strong rival and a credible alternative for the viewer's budget; the short video can emphasize fewer comparisons than the source sheet. The brief records editorial reasoning; the renderer cannot establish whether a hook is good.

For this growth-oriented channel, also record the angle decision from [attention and payoff](attention-and-payoff.md): what the audience gains beyond the headline, the opening/first-frame pairing, where its promise is paid off, and a concrete reason to save/share or act. This is editorial reasoning, not a required new CLI schema or measured engagement. Do not manufacture platform statistics or make a weak topic seem stronger by hiding qualifiers.

## Gather visuals and produce

Collect official release posters, charts, interface pages and relevant publicly viewable examples. Record page URLs, figure/demo identifiers, rights/usage context, hashes and the time ranges used. Brief source-page/demo excerpts used for reporting must be attributed and distinguish `官方演示 / 官方自测` from the agent's own successful test. A public demo need not be installed or purchased merely to report its launch. Do not invent a demonstration, generated output or a benchmark.

Prefer topic-specific examples for the main footage; use contextual licensed stock sparingly and label it. General footage of typing and a TTS terminal cannot substitute for the chosen model's announcement, examples and evidence. Real operation is required when the user asks for a test/demo; news coverage may use attributed official examples. Original animated charts explain the verified facts and must not impersonate a vendor UI.

Select media after deciding the argument. Map each planned shot to the claim it proves or explains. With this user's source-led preference, recorded inspections and close screenshots of the original comparison/pricing evidence form the main picture; use short callouts/highlights to explain full model versions, units and source conditions. Original data graphics can supplement an unclear comparison, but do not replace the source material for most of the runtime. Avoid unrelated landscapes, source-video establishing shots that hide the result, and repeating the same demo to fill unrelated price/benchmark sections. Show a source page's relevant row, figure or paragraph at a readable scale instead of lingering on an entire announcement. Watch the recorded segment itself, including the beginning and end, before choosing time ranges. Official production demos are examples of that model's workflow, not automatically proof that every asset was generated from scratch.

Write original Chinese narration and shot timing, synthesize with the selected voice (on this host Ming C / 二次元细声), assemble captions and edit locally, then create the cover and publishing copy. Respect the user's requested delivery stage; an existing request for a finished video authorizes production without another confirmation. A draft-first request still stops at the draft.

Inspect the encoded video, topic/visual consistency, subtitle readability, voice omissions and source attributions. Deliver MP4, cover, SRT, publishing copy, research/selection note, source/material manifest and actual verification. No paid APIs or account publishing are implied.
